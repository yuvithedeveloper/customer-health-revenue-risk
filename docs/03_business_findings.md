# Business Findings & Recommendations

## Executive Summary

CloudPath Analytics currently has 426 active customer accounts representing approximately $18.90M in annual recurring revenue (ARR). The customer health analysis identifies 153 accounts that require Customer Success attention, including 24 At Risk accounts and 129 Monitor accounts.

These accounts represent approximately $6.64M in gross ARR exposure. Using the scenario-based revenue exposure model, approximately $2.32M is estimated to be at risk.

The analysis suggests that product adoption and usage behavior are the strongest indicators of customer risk, while retention performance also varies significantly by customer segment. Enterprise customers show stronger revenue retention than Mid-Market and SMB customers, while support demand has increased over the analysis period.

---

## Key Finding 1 — A meaningful portion of ARR requires Customer Success attention

Of the 426 active customer accounts:

- 273 are Healthy
- 129 are Monitor
- 24 are At Risk

This means 153 accounts, or approximately 36% of the active customer base, require some level of Customer Success attention.

These accounts represent approximately $6.64M in ARR exposure, while the estimated Revenue at Risk is approximately $2.32M.

### Business implication

Customer Success resources should not be distributed evenly across all accounts. The health and priority models provide a way to concentrate intervention efforts on the accounts where deterioration could create the greatest revenue impact.

### Recommendation

Use the customer priority score as the starting point for weekly Customer Success reviews. Critical and High priority accounts should receive proactive outreach first, while Medium and Low priority accounts can be managed through structured follow-up workflows.

---

## Key Finding 2 — Product Usage is the largest customer risk driver

Product Usage is the primary risk driver for 90 of the 153 accounts requiring action.

Estimated Revenue at Risk associated with Product Usage is approximately $1.46M, substantially higher than the other identified risk drivers.

Other primary risk drivers include:

- Usage Trend: 25 accounts and approximately $0.41M Revenue at Risk
- Customer Engagement: 22 accounts and approximately $0.20M Revenue at Risk
- Support Health: 11 accounts and approximately $0.20M Revenue at Risk
- Payment Health: 5 accounts and approximately $0.06M Revenue at Risk

### Business implication

Customer risk is being driven more by how customers use the platform than by payment issues or support activity alone.

A customer may continue paying while product usage declines, which makes behavioral signals useful as an earlier warning indicator.

### Recommendation

Create proactive adoption interventions for accounts with low active-user rates, weak feature adoption, or declining usage trends.

Customer Success teams should investigate whether these customers require:

- additional onboarding,
- user training,
- feature education,
- workflow redesign,
- or executive engagement.

---

## Key Finding 3 — 48 accounts require urgent Customer Success attention

The prioritization model identifies 48 accounts in the Critical or High priority tiers.

These accounts combine multiple factors such as:

- poor health scores,
- revenue exposure,
- renewal timing,
- and account value.

The highest-priority accounts therefore represent more than simply the lowest health scores.

### Business implication

A health score alone is not enough to determine intervention priority.

For example, an account with moderate health but high ARR and an upcoming renewal may require action before a lower-value account with a worse health score.

### Recommendation

Use the priority score rather than the health score alone when deciding the order of Customer Success outreach.

The operational workflow should begin with Critical accounts, followed by High, Medium, and Low priority accounts.

---

## Key Finding 4 — Revenue risk is spread across different renewal horizons

Estimated Revenue at Risk by renewal window is approximately:

- 0–30 days: $0.13M
- 31–60 days: $0.18M
- 61–90 days: $0.28M
- 91–180 days: $0.42M
- 181–270 days: $0.65M
- 271+ days: $0.66M

Although near-term renewals require immediate action, a large portion of exposed revenue belongs to customers with more than six months remaining before renewal.

### Business implication

Waiting until an account enters a 30- or 60-day renewal window would be too late for many customers.

The company has time to influence a large amount of exposed revenue before renewal pressure becomes immediate.

### Recommendation

Separate Customer Success intervention into two tracks:

1. **Immediate renewal protection** for accounts renewing within 90 days.
2. **Preventive adoption programs** for high-risk customers with longer renewal horizons.

This allows the team to protect near-term revenue while also improving future retention.

## Key Finding 5 — 2026 retention weakened compared with 2025

Overall retention performance declined between 2025 and 2026.

### 2025

- Logo Retention: 91.67%
- Gross Revenue Retention (GRR): 94.05%
- Net Revenue Retention (NRR): 99.27%

### 2026

- Logo Retention: 90.35%
- GRR: 92.47%
- NRR: 97.42%

Expansion MRR increased substantially in 2026, but churned MRR also increased.

### Business implication

Customer expansion is partially offsetting churn and contraction, but not enough to push NRR above 100%.

This suggests that the company is successfully expanding some relationships while losing too much recurring revenue from others.

### Recommendation

Management should evaluate retention and expansion separately.

Expansion programs should continue, but they should not mask deterioration in the existing customer base. Customer Success performance should therefore monitor GRR, NRR, and Logo Retention together rather than relying on NRR alone.

---

## Key Finding 6 — Enterprise customers have materially stronger retention

2026 retention differs significantly by segment.

### Enterprise

- GRR: 97.70%
- NRR: 103.54%

### Mid-Market

- GRR: 89.47%
- NRR: 93.79%

### SMB

- GRR: 87.16%
- NRR: 91.47%

Enterprise is the only segment with NRR above 100%, meaning expansion more than offsets churn and contraction within the starting Enterprise customer cohort.

### Business implication

The retention problem is concentrated more heavily in Mid-Market and SMB customers.

Enterprise customers appear both more resilient and more capable of generating expansion revenue.

### Recommendation

Investigate which Enterprise Customer Success practices can be adapted for Mid-Market customers.

For SMB accounts, the company may need a more scalable retention model using automated onboarding, product education, lifecycle campaigns, and usage-triggered outreach rather than high-touch account management.

---

## Key Finding 7 — Mid-Market generated the largest 2026 churned MRR

Approximately:

- Mid-Market churned MRR: $46K
- SMB churned MRR: $29K
- Enterprise churned MRR: $9K

Although SMB has weaker retention percentages, Mid-Market creates the greatest absolute recurring-revenue loss.

### Business implication

Percentage-based retention metrics and absolute revenue loss tell different stories.

SMB has the weakest retention rate, but Mid-Market churn creates more direct revenue impact.

### Recommendation

Prioritize Mid-Market churn reduction for direct financial impact while developing scalable retention improvements for SMB.

This prevents the company from focusing exclusively on either percentages or absolute revenue values.

---

## Key Finding 8 — Usage recovered, but support pressure increased

By December 2026:

- Active User Rate is approximately 65.4%
- Feature Adoption is approximately 72.7%
- Average support resolution time is approximately 27.1 hours
- Average CSAT is approximately 4.24

Usage declined during portions of 2025 before recovering during 2026.

At the same time, total support-ticket volume and High/Critical ticket volume generally increased over the analysis period.

### Business implication

Improving product adoption is positive, but higher usage may also create greater support demand.

If support capacity does not scale with customer activity, increased product engagement could eventually create a service-quality problem.

### Recommendation

Monitor product adoption and support demand together.

Management should track whether increasing usage is accompanied by:

- increased ticket volume,
- more severe tickets,
- longer resolution times,
- or declining CSAT.

Support staffing, self-service resources, and product documentation should scale alongside customer adoption.

# Recommended Customer Success Action Plan

Based on the analysis, CloudPath Analytics should use a structured intervention strategy:

1. Prioritize the 48 Critical and High priority accounts for immediate review.
2. Protect accounts renewing within the next 90 days through direct Customer Success outreach.
3. Launch proactive adoption programs for accounts whose primary risk driver is Product Usage or Usage Trend.
4. Focus Mid-Market retention initiatives on reducing absolute recurring-revenue loss.
5. Develop scalable retention programs for SMB customers.
6. Study Enterprise retention and expansion practices for approaches that can be adapted to other segments.
7. Review support capacity as customer usage grows.
8. Track health score, Revenue at Risk, GRR, NRR, Logo Retention, and renewal exposure as part of a recurring Customer Success operating review.

---

# Analytical Limitation

This project uses synthetic data designed to simulate realistic B2B SaaS customer behavior. Therefore, the findings should be interpreted as the output of an analytical business simulation rather than conclusions about a real company.

The Estimated Revenue at Risk metric is also a scenario-based prioritization measure. It represents modeled revenue exposure based on customer health rather than a guaranteed forecast of future churn.
